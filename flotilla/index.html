How it works

From idea to merged PR,
without losing track.

1 Start

A new agent is one keystroke away.

Press ⌘N, pick a project, describe the task. Flotilla names the session for you with Apple's on-device model, so you never invent another branch name.

⌘N
2 Isolate

Every agent gets its own checkout.

Agents work in their own git worktrees, drawn from a pool of ready slots. Ten agents can work in the same repo without stepping on each other's files.

Flotilla keeps them honest too: an agent that tries to make a second checkout gets stopped and pointed back to its own.

3 Arrange

Tile, drag, zoom. All from the keyboard.

Drop agents from the sidebar into the grid, split panes in any direction and zoom in on the one that matters. Smart Organize lays them all out evenly in one key.

⌥D⌥R⌥↩⌥S
4 Respond

Know the moment someone needs you.

Blocked agents light up in the sidebar and send a notification. Approve once, or turn on auto-approve for agents you trust.

Walk away for lunch: Flotilla keeps your Mac awake while agents are working.

5 Ship

Review and merge without leaving the app.

Each agent's pull requests sit right next to its terminal, with CI checks and review state updating live. When it merges, delete the session and its checkout in one go.

Features

Everything around the agent.
Nothing in its way.

Flotilla doesn't replace your agent or your terminal. It's the cockpit around them: layout, status, checkouts, review and scheduling.

Agents outlive the app Pro

Quit, update or crash Flotilla and your agents keep running in the background. Open it again and every pane is right where you left it.

            Routines
            
Agents that show up on schedule.

            
Give a prompt a schedule in plain words, like "every 4h" or "weekdays 9:00". Each run is a normal agent you can open and watch, and it leaves a one-line summary in the history.

          

          
    
            
  • Asks before it acts. A run can stop and ask you to approve or pick an option. Your answer goes straight back to it.
    • 
            
  • One run at a time. Slow runs never pile up, and stuck ones are stopped at a timeout.
    • 
            
  • Cleans up after itself. A finished run removes its checkout, unless that would lose work.
